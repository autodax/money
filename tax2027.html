Assumptions & methodology

2027 IRS figures (Rev. Proc. 2026-35): $16,600 single standard deduction; seven ordinary brackets (10/12/22/24/32/35/37%). Qualified dividends & LTCG stack on top of ordinary taxable income at 0/15/20%; short-term gains taxed as ordinary income. NIIT's $200,000 MAGI threshold is fixed by statute, not inflation-adjusted. "65 or older" adds the $2,100 age-65 deduction plus the temporary senior bonus deduction (up to $6,000, phased out 6%/dollar above $75,000 MAGI, gone by $175,000; 2025–2028 only). Federal-only estimate — excludes payroll tax, state tax, and credits.

How this is calculated

This splits the same taxes shown elsewhere on the page, so the rows add up to the total. Ordinary tax is layered through the brackets from the bottom: wages first, then taxable Social Security, interest and ordinary dividends, short-term gains, and the IRA withdrawal / Roth conversion last, so it carries the highest ordinary bracket rate. The standard deduction is applied to the lowest layers first. Qualified dividends and long-term gains are taxed at 0/15/20% on top of ordinary taxable income, and the 3.8% NIIT is shared across dividends and gains in proportion to each. The employer estimate is tax on wages alone with the standard deduction; your actual withholding depends on your withholding settings in Oracle HR, pay frequency, and bonuses.

How this is calculated

Medicare published the 2027 premiums and tiers on November 17, 2026: standard Part B premium $209.40/month with no IRMAA. Part D surcharge is paid on top of your plan's own premium, direct to Medicare. It's a cliff — one dollar over moves the whole year to the next tier. Uses the 2025 MAGI entered at left.

How this is calculated

IRMAA for 2029 is set by your 2027 MAGI, which here is your total income above (AGI, including the taxable part of Social Security; tax-exempt interest isn't tracked). Medicare hasn't published 2029 thresholds or premiums yet, so this uses the 2027 tier table and premiums as a stand-in. Thresholds are normally indexed upward each year, so the actual tier may be the same or lower. Not included in the 2027 tax totals.
